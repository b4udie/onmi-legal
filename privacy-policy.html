Onmi Legal

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 26 September 2026 · Last updated: 26 September 2026

1. Who we are

Onmi (bundle identifier com.onmi.b4udie) is an iOS application for keeping a digital wardrobe, putting outfits together and trying them on virtually with the help of AI. It is published by Valentyn Bratkevych, an individual developer (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what personal data the app handles, why, and what you can do about it. It applies to the app only, not to any other product or website.

For data protection purposes, Valentyn Bratkevych is the controller of the personal data described below. You can reach us at bratkevychv@proton.me.

2. The short version

  • You sign in with Apple. We do not ask Apple for your name or email address.
  • The photos you add — clothes, outfits and a photo of yourself — are sent to our servers so AI models can turn them into wardrobe items and try-on images.
  • Your photos and results are kept until you delete them or delete your account. There is no automatic deletion by time.
  • You can delete your account inside the app, and doing so deletes your photos, wardrobe, looks and try-on results.
  • We do not use analytics or advertising SDKs, we do not track you across other apps or websites, and we do not sell your data.
  • You can ask us what we hold about you and ask us to delete it.

3. Information we collect

3.1 Information you provide

  • Account details — when you sign in with Apple, Apple gives us a stable user identifier for Onmi. The app does not request your name or your email address, so we do not receive them.
  • Photos of clothes and outfits — photos you take with the camera or pick from your library to add items to your wardrobe, including outfit photos that may show you or another person wearing the clothes.
  • Your try-on photo — a full-length photo of yourself that the app uses to show outfits on you. It shows your face and body.
  • Wardrobe and looks — the items in your wardrobe and what we or you record about them (category, name, colour and similar attributes), the looks you put together, and the try-on images generated for them.
  • Feedback on results — if you rate a try-on result (for example thumbs down with a reason), we store that rating with the result.
  • Support messages — if you email us, we receive your address and whatever you write.

3.2 Information collected automatically

  • Technical request data — when the app talks to our servers we process the request itself, the app version, and the technical information every internet request carries, such as the IP address and the operating system version. We use it to deliver the service, keep it compatible with your app version and prevent abuse, not to build a profile of you.
  • Usage records needed for your plan — how many outfit photos you processed this month, whether you used the free try-on, your credit balance and the history of credits added and spent.
  • Push notification token — if you allow notifications, the token Apple issues for your device (see section 9).
  • Purchase status — whether a subscription or credit pack purchase is active or completed, and the transaction identifiers Apple and RevenueCat give us. We never receive your card number or billing details.

3.3 Information from third parties

Apple tells us the identifier described above when you sign in, and Apple (through RevenueCat, our purchase service provider) tells us whether a purchase succeeded and whether a subscription is active or was refunded. Aside from that, we do not buy or receive personal data about you from anyone.

4. How your photos are processed

Photos are the heart of the app, so here is exactly what happens to them.

  • On your device first. When you add a single item, the app cuts the garment out of the photo on your iPhone using Apple’s on-device Vision framework. When you add a try-on photo of yourself, the app checks on your iPhone, again with Apple Vision, that the photo shows exactly one person, full length, with a visible face. These checks run entirely on your device; only the photo you then choose to save is uploaded.
  • On our servers. Uploaded photos are stored privately and processed by AI models to recognise and describe clothing, separate individual items, produce clean product-style images of them, and generate try-on images of your outfits on your photo. The models are open-source models that we run on GPU servers rented from RunPod. They are not provided by an AI company that receives your data for its own purposes.
  • Very small accessories. When an item is tiny in the photo (for example a watch or a pendant), we send a cropped section of the photo around that item to Replicate, which runs Google’s image model “Nano Banana” to produce the product image. That crop can include a small part of the body wearing the item, such as a wrist or neck. No other photos are sent to Replicate or Google.
  • Your face. To keep your face looking like you in try-on results, our server locates the face on your try-on photo and on the generated image and carries the original face region over. This locates a face in an image; it does not identify who you are, and we do not create or keep face recognition templates or any biometric identifiers.
  • No automated moderation and no human review by default. We do not run automated content moderation on your photos, and we do not look at them, except when needed to investigate a problem you report to us, to act on a report of unlawful content, or to comply with the law.
  • No model training. We do not use your photos or results to train AI models, and we have no agreement allowing our service providers to train on them.

Please upload photos of other people only if they have agreed to it (see the Terms of Use).

The app asks for a permission only when a feature needs it, and iOS shows you the reason at that moment. You can withdraw any permission in Settings › Onmi; the feature that relied on it stops working, the rest of the app keeps working. Picking photos from your library uses the system photo picker, which gives the app only the photos you select, without access to the rest of your library.

8. Purchases and subscriptions

Purchases are processed by Apple through the App Store, and we use RevenueCat to keep track of them. Apple handles payment and receives your payment details; we receive only the purchase status and transaction identifiers, which is what lets the app unlock Pro, add credits and restore purchases on a new device. Apple’s handling of your payment data is governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy.

9. Notifications

Push notifications are off until you allow them. If you allow them, Apple issues a device token that we store with your account and use only to tell you that a try-on is ready. Turn them off at any time in Settings › Notifications › Onmi.

10. Tracking, analytics and advertising

The app contains no advertising, analytics or crash reporting SDKs, does not read Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA), does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites, and does not build advertising profiles. Because of that, the app never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt.

11. Sharing and disclosure

We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. Your photos and results are not visible to other users. We disclose data only to the service providers listed above, when the law requires it (a valid legal request), to protect our rights or someone’s safety, or to a successor if the app changes hands — in which case this policy continues to apply until you are told otherwise.

12. International transfers

Our API servers and database run in Frankfurt, Germany, and photos are stored with Cloudflare in Europe. Some providers process data elsewhere: RunPod GPU servers may be located in the United States or other countries, and Replicate, Google, RevenueCat and Apple operate from the United States. Where data leaves the European Economic Area, the transfer relies on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses or another lawful transfer mechanism offered by the provider. Ask us at bratkevychv@proton.me if you want the specifics for a given provider.

13. How long we keep data

  • Photos, try-on photo, wardrobe items, looks and try-on results are kept until you delete them in the app or delete your account. There is no automatic deletion after a period of time.
  • When you delete a single item, photo, look or result, its image files are erased from storage straight away. A minimal database record that it existed (without the image) may remain until you delete your account.
  • When you delete your account, we delete your account record and everything linked to it from the database, erase all of your files from storage, revoke the app’s Sign in with Apple authorisation and ask RevenueCat to delete your customer record. If deleted data is contained in a backup, it disappears when that backup is overwritten.
  • Purchase event records that we receive about App Store transactions are kept after account deletion, detached from your account, for accounting, refunds and fraud prevention.
  • Server logs with technical request data are kept only for a limited time for security and troubleshooting.
  • Support emails are kept as long as needed to handle your request.

14. Security

Traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Photos are kept in private storage and are reachable only through short-lived signed links (typically valid for 15 minutes). Access to systems that hold personal data is limited to the developer. No system is perfectly secure, so we keep the amount of data we hold as small as the app allows.

15. Your rights and choices

  • Access — ask what personal data we hold about you.
  • Correction — ask us to fix data that is wrong.
  • Deletion — delete individual photos, items, looks and results in the app, delete your account, or ask us to delete your data.
  • Objection and restriction — object to processing based on legitimate interests, or ask us to pause it.
  • Portability — receive a copy of the data you gave us in a machine-readable format.
  • Withdraw consent — where processing is based on consent (notifications), withdraw it at any time; this does not affect what happened before.

Write to bratkevychv@proton.me and we will respond within 30 days. We may need to verify that the request comes from you.

Deleting your account. Open Settings › Delete account in the app and confirm. This deletes the personal data associated with your account as described in section 13, except records we must keep by law. No email to us is required. Deleting your account does not cancel an App Store subscription — cancel it in Settings › your Apple Account › Subscriptions.

If you are in the EEA or the UK and you think we have handled your data wrongly, you may also complain to your national data protection authority. If you are in Ukraine, you may complain to the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights.

16. Children

The app is not directed to children, and you must be at least 16 years old to use it. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 16. If you believe a child has provided us with personal data, email bratkevychv@proton.me and we will delete it.
